AI-Provider Router IDE vs. the market

A side-by-side comparison of our local router + gateway against seven leading alternatives, across performance, features, pricing, protocol compatibility, security and maturity. Every number for our own product was measured against the running gateway, not read from documentation.

Product v1.2.0 · Apache-2.0 ◈ Gateway benchmarked live 2026-09-27 Loopback 127.0.0.1:8800 Competitor data: vendor docs & analyst sources, Sept 2026 8 options compared
How to read this. Two provenance classes are kept strictly apart. Measured figures come from probes run against the live gateway on this machine (150 iterations per endpoint, percentiles computed). Reported figures come from vendor documentation, published benchmarks and 2026 analyst reviews — they are cited, not reproduced as if we had verified them. Where our own product could not be measured on a comparable basis, the comparison says so rather than extrapolating.
Correction to the first version of this document. OmniRoute was omitted and has been added. It is not a peripheral entry — it is the closest competitor on the list, and its omission understated our exposure. It is local-first, MIT, ships a desktop app, targets exactly our user, and at 70,689 stars in 226 days (≈313 stars/day) it has already overtaken LiteLLM. Its RTK+Caveman compression claims to cut prompt tokens by 15–95% (≈89% average) — which attacks our single largest measured weakness head-on: the ≈1,719 prompt tokens of fixed context every one of our requests carries.

One line each. The full evidence follows in sections 03–07.

Local-only, zero egress
Binds 127.0.0.1, keys in a mode-600 file, no vendor account. Shared with OmniRoute and a self-hosted LiteLLM — but neither ships a four-dialect inbound surface or a built-in memory layer.
Closest competitor
OmniRoute
Not LiteLLM. OmniRoute is local-first, MIT, desktop, agent-focused and free — the same niche. 359 providers, 17–19 routing strategies, MCP/A2A, token compression. Revision to the first draft of this page, which named LiteLLM.
Largest gap vs. market
No release, no users
Zero adoption while OmniRoute adds ~313 stars/day. No downloadable build, macOS-only in practice, no multi-user story. Breadth is not our binding constraint — distribution is.

Caveat — do not read this as "we are 40× faster." The two measurements are not like-for-like. Ours is a single-user loopback listener with one provider configured and no upstream call; LiteLLM's is a Python proxy sustained at >1,000 RPS across 2–4 instances with Postgres and streaming enabled. What the comparison legitimately shows is that our architecture is not the bottleneck — the router adds well under a millisecond at the median, so end-to-end latency is essentially the provider's. Our throughput ceiling remains unverified at load: live traffic has never reached the 8-concurrent / 32-queued gate.
The performance question OmniRoute reframes. Raw routing overhead is not where this contest is decided. OmniRoute's RTK+Caveman compression claims 15–95% token savings (≈89% average) — unverified, with no published dataset or reproduction — while we add ≈1,719 prompt tokens of fixed context to every single request, measured. If even a fraction of that claim holds, OmniRoute is cheaper per request by an order of magnitude on the axis that actually scales with usage. Reducing our fixed context overhead is the highest-leverage performance work available to us, and it is cheap: it is prompt assembly, not architecture.

End-to-end, with the provider call included: a live non-streaming chat returned 200 in 2.64 s and a streaming request had 23 ms to first byte.

05 Advantages and disadvantages, option by option

Honest ledger. Our own column lists gaps first, because those are the ones that decide whether to keep building. OmniRoute is listed second — it is the one to beat.

AI-Provider Router IDE
ours · v1.2.0 · Apache-2.0 · $0

Advantages

  • Lowest measured overhead — sub-millisecond median on gateway-local paths.
  • Truly local. Loopback bind, mode-600 secrets file, no vendor account, no telemetry.
  • Four inbound dialects including Anthropic and Gemini, verified live — nobody else here does this.
  • Built-in scoped memory layer (BM25/FTS5) with a 15 ms deadline; OmniRoute's is an optional Qdrant sidecar.
  • Resilience primitives are real and measured: 6 attempts, 1 s cooldown floor, half-open breaker, 500 ms→32 s backoff.
  • Security posture is conservative — no fail-open guardrails, no default-secret problem.
  • Provider-agnostic onboarding — probe→identify→test wizard for unknown APIs.

Disadvantages

  • Zero adoption. No release build; needs Apple notarisation. No reviews exist.
  • macOS-only in practice — CI is macos-14; Win/Linux untested. OmniRoute runs everywhere.
  • ≈1,719 prompt tokens/request of fixed overhead, billed every call. OmniRoute compresses.
  • Single-user. No RBAC, no roles, no user identity, no multi-tenant story.
  • No response or semantic cache — every call is billed again.
  • No MCP/A2A — OmniRoute has 95 MCP tools.
  • No embeddings, audio or rerank; image route has no tagged model today.
  • No Docker, no cloud, no TLS — cannot be deployed for a team.
  • No compliance posture — no SOC 2 / HIPAA.
  • Throughput ceiling unverified at load.
Best for: a single developer on macOS who wants one local endpoint for every provider, with keys that never leave the machine.
OmniRoute — closest rival
MIT · 70,689★ · 359 providers · TypeScript

Advantages

  • Enormous momentum — 70,689★ in 226 days (≈313/day); already past LiteLLM.
  • 359 providers, 150+ with free tiers, 1,200+ models. Breadth we cannot match.
  • Token compression (RTK+Caveman) — claimed 15–95%. Attacks our fixed-context cost directly.
  • 17–19 routing strategies with quota-aware auto-fallback.
  • MCP/A2A with 95 tools; works with Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Cline, Copilot and 26+ more.
  • Cross-platform — Node.js and Docker, desktop app and PWA.
  • Free forever, MIT, zero telemetry by default.

Disadvantages

  • Security maturity rated lowest in class. Default JWT_SECRET is bypassable unrotated; guardrails described as fail-open.
  • Free-tier aggregation is a ToS grey area. Many free tiers are personal-use only; multi-account use breaches most platforms. Account-ban risk is real.
  • Compression is lossy — ~89% average may degrade precision reasoning and context-sensitive code.
  • No published overhead, throughput or SLA. No compliance certs; reviewers advise against customer-facing production.
  • Catalog churn — provider count moved 231→359 in months; not a stable contract.
  • Compose ships Redis without requirepass; dashboard is LAN-reachable.
  • No native Anthropic/Gemini inbound dialect; no built-in scoped memory.
Best for: a developer who wants maximum free capacity and breadth, and accepts the terms-of-service and quality risk.
LiteLLM
BerriAI · MIT · 59,710★ · Python

Advantages

  • 100+ providers out of the box; de facto standard.
  • Fully self-hostable — data stays in your VPC.
  • Zero markup; per-team/user/model budgets in Postgres.
  • Redis semantic caching and cost/latency-based routing.
  • Python extensibility — native to AI teams.
  • Huge community; very active development.

Disadvantages

  • You own operations — uptime, upgrades, observability.
  • 5,319 open issues — breadth comes with instability.
  • Highest overhead of the measured set (12 ms p50 at 1k RPS).
  • High memory usage at init and per request.
  • No persistent memory layer; no desktop GUI.
Best for: engineering teams that want control, zero markup, and will operate infrastructure.
OpenRouter
managed only · 400+ models

Advantages

  • Fastest possible start — one key, hundreds of models.
  • Automatic provider fallbacks, zero infrastructure.
  • Largest single catalog (400+ models).
  • Free tier to begin; no ops burden at all.

Disadvantages

  • No self-host option — every request transits a third party.
  • 5.5% credit fee (min $0.80); ~8% on the Business tier.
  • Least routing control — fallbacks are opaque by design.
  • Thinnest deep observability; unsuitable for data-sensitive workloads.

Portkey
MIT · 13,091★ · SaaS + OSS

Advantages

  • Strongest governance story — RBAC, audit trails, guardrails, compliance-ready.
  • Conditional, content-aware routing and fine-grained fallback chains.
  • Native observability — request tracing, prompt management.
  • Self-hostable open-source gateway.
  • Low memory footprint; CPU capped ~40% under load.

Disadvantages

  • Plan caps bite — 10K req/mo free; $49/mo for 100K; $9 per 100K overage.
  • SaaS-centric — full features shine only on the managed platform.
  • OSS gateway slower to move — last pushed 2026-05-25.
  • No persistent memory layer.
Best for: organisations that must satisfy compliance review and want governance without building it.
Kong AI Gateway
Kong Inc. · OSS / Konnect / Enterprise

Advantages

  • ~10 ms latency, 350+ RPS on 1 vCPU — proven data-plane performance.
  • Mature enterprise platform — unified control plane for all traffic.
  • Self-hostable; strong legacy protocol support.
  • Right choice if you already run Kong Enterprise.

Disadvantages

  • Most expensive. ≈$105/mo per gateway service + ≈$34.25/1M requests + ≈$720/mo infra; Enterprise often >$50k/yr.
  • Per-request billing punishes agentic workloads — one agent turn can be 20+ calls.
  • Lua extensibility — a real skills gap for Python-first AI teams.
  • Token-level limiting is a paid plugin. Heavy footprint for LLM-only use.
Best for: existing Kong Enterprise shops consolidating AI traffic onto one control plane.
Cloudflare AI Gateway
managed edge · core free

Advantages

  • Core features free with no per-call fee — cheapest viable managed option.
  • Very cheap at volume — ≈$8/mo for ~15M requests on Workers Paid.
  • Free prompt/response caching, rate limiting, analytics.
  • Edge DDoS/WAF protection included.

Disadvantages

  • Managed only — all traffic transits Cloudflare.
  • Log caps are hard stops — 100K logs/mo free, 1M paid, ~10M storage per gateway; no paid overage.
  • Free tier limited to 100K requests/day and eligible models only.
  • Workers ecosystem lock-in; no memory layer.
Best for: cost-sensitive teams already on Cloudflare who accept third-party transit.
Vercel AI Gateway
managed · zero markup

Advantages

  • Zero markup — provider list price, BYOK included.
  • No fixed monthly fee; $5/mo in included credits.

Disadvantages

  • Managed only — data transits Vercel.
  • Free tier restricted to eligible models with lower rate limits.
  • Team add-ons need Vercel Pro at $20/user/mo; Enterprise ≈$25k+/yr.
  • Free credits stop once you purchase credits; ecosystem lock-in.
Best for: Vercel-native teams that want zero markup without operating a proxy.

06 Which to choose

A decision rule, not a ranking. The right answer depends on which constraint is binding.

If data must never leave your machine
Ours, OmniRoute, or self-hosted LiteLLM
All three run locally. Ours needs no server and never leaves loopback; OmniRoute is local but routes prompts to whichever provider has quota, including free tiers; LiteLLM stays in your VPC.
If squeezing cost toward zero matters most
OmniRoute
150+ free providers stitched automatically, plus compression. Accept the ToS grey area, lossy compression and ban risk — and keep it out of anything customer-facing.
If you need a team, roles and audit
Portkey
The only option here with RBAC and compliance-ready logging out of the box. LiteLLM can reach the same bar, but you assemble it yourself.
If you want breadth with no ops
OpenRouter
One key, hundreds of models, automatic fallbacks. Accept the 5.5% credit fee and third-party transit; migrate to self-host when the markup compounds.
If cost at scale is the binding constraint
Cloudflare AI Gateway
No per-call fee and roughly $8/mo at ~15M requests. Watch the log-retention caps — they are hard stops, not overages.
If you are one developer on macOS
Ours — with eyes open
Sub-millisecond routing, four API dialects, a real memory layer, a GUI, and keys in a mode-600 file. OmniRoute beats us on breadth, platform reach and cost; we beat it on dialect coverage, built-in memory and security conservatism.
Revised positioning. An earlier draft of this page argued our niche was "zero-ops single-machine with a memory layer" and named LiteLLM as the sharpest competitor. OmniRoute occupies that exact niche, ships it cross-platform, and is growing at ~313 stars/day. Our remaining differentiators are narrower than the draft implied: four inbound dialects (they have OpenAI + responses), built-in scoped memory (they have an optional Qdrant sidecar), measured sub-millisecond overhead (they publish nothing), and conservative security (theirs is rated fail-open with a bypassable default secret). Those are real, but they are refinements — not a category. The binding constraints on us are unchanged and now more urgent: no distributable release, macOS-only, and ≈1,719 tokens of fixed context per request.
